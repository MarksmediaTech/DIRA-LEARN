### 5. Step-by-step teaching content
#### 0:00–0:10 · Welcome to Content Creation and track overview (10 min, introduction and recap)
*Meet your track group and see the six sessions and the final portfolio.*

1. Show the journey: S3 strategy, storytelling and formats, S4 photography, lighting, composition, mobile video and sound capture, S5 video editing, audio and captions, S6 design, branding, visual storytelling and copyright, S7 portfolio and project build, S8 showcase.
2. Show an example portfolio (fictional creator 'Otieno Shoots', a Kisumu creator making short videos for local food spots). Say that by session 8 each learner will have their own.
3. Ask learners to name a Kenyan creator they follow and why they keep watching.

#### 0:10–0:30 · Content strategy and planning (20 min, main lesson)
*Learn how to define purpose, audience, niche, pillars and rhythm for content.*

1. Purpose: why are you creating - to build a personal creator brand, to promote a business, to educate a community, or to tell stories for a cause? Purpose decides everything else.
2. Audience: who exactly? 'Students in Eldoret who want to eat well on a budget' is stronger than 'young people'.
3. Niche: what you want to be known for. A niche can be a topic (budget food), a style (funny skits in Sheng), or a place (Kisumu). Broad creators struggle to grow; focused creators get remembered.
4. Goals: followers are not the only goal. Others include enquiries for a business, sign-ups for an event, saves and shares (people found it useful), or paid briefs.
5. Pillars and rhythm: three or four pillars (for example 'cheap eats reviews', 'cook with me', 'campus food news'). A rhythm you can keep for three months (for example three short videos a week) beats a burst followed by silence.
6. Creator content versus brand content: creators speak as themselves; brand content speaks for a business and follows its brand. Many creators earn by making brand content for clients - the portfolio will show both.

#### 0:30–0:50 · Storytelling (20 min, main lesson)
*Learn story structures that hold attention, with local examples.*

1. The hook: the first 2 seconds decide whether people keep watching. Hooks - a surprising visual, a question ('Can you eat lunch for KES 100 in town?'), a bold statement, a result first ('This KES 500 room makeover...').
2. Story arc: setup (who and where), tension (a problem, a question or a challenge), payoff (the answer, the result, the emotion), call to action (follow, comment, visit).
3. Character, conflict and change: people connect with people. A mama mboga starting her day at 4 am at Marikiti market and selling out by noon is a story; 'we sell vegetables' is not.
4. Show, don't tell: instead of saying 'our tailor is skilled', show hands cutting fabric, a fitting and the client's smile.
5. Authenticity and language: speak like your audience - English, Kiswahili, Sheng or your local language - as long as your target audience understands. Add captions so more people can follow.
6. Play or describe two short examples and ask learners to identify the hook, tension and payoff.

#### 0:50–1:05 · Content formats for different platforms (15 min, main lesson)
*Match formats and specifications to platforms and audiences.*

1. Short vertical video (TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Facebook Reels): 9:16 (1080 x 1920), usually 7 to 60 seconds for discovery content, strong hook, captions, trending or original audio.
2. Feed photos and carousels (Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn): 1:1 (1080 x 1080) or 4:5 (1080 x 1350); carousels tell step-by-step or before-and-after stories.
3. Stories and WhatsApp status: vertical, short-lived, informal - good for behind the scenes and daily updates.
4. Long-form video (YouTube): 16:9 (1920 x 1080), for tutorials, vlogs and interviews; thumbnail and title matter.
5. Audio and podcasts: interviews and conversations; good sound is essential.
6. Text-led platforms (LinkedIn, X): opinions, lessons and professional updates, often with an image or video.
7. Platform specifications and limits change; check each platform's current help pages before publishing.

*A five-minute stretch and movement break (seated option available).*

1. Invite everyone to stand, stretch, walk or drink water for five minutes; offer a seated option for anyone who prefers or needs it.
2. Restart on time with a one-line reminder of where the session is and what comes next.

#### 1:10–1:25 · Demonstration - from idea to storyboard (15 min, demonstration)
*Watch a story idea turned into a six-frame storyboard.*

1. Take an idea: 'A day with a boda boda rider who delivers lunch in Thika'. Write the hook, tension and payoff.
2. Draw a six-frame storyboard: frame number, shot type (wide, medium, close-up), what we see, what we hear (voice, sound, music), on-screen text, duration. Total 30 to 45 seconds.
3. Show how the storyboard becomes a shot list for filming in session 4.

## Session 4 (Week 2, 120 minutes): Smartphone photography, lighting, composition and mobile video

*Take clear, well-lit, well-composed photos and short videos on a phone, and capture clean sound.*

### 1. Learning purpose
Learners master the phone as a camera. They learn composition rules, how to use natural light, camera settings that matter, shot types for video, stable filming and clean sound capture, then shoot a photo set and the clips for their storyboard.

### 2. Learning objectives
By the end of the session learners can:

- Film stable shots in the right orientation using wide, medium and close-up shots and B-roll
- Capture clean audio (quiet location, mic close to the speaker, avoiding wind and echo)
- Shoot a five-photo set and the clips for their storyboard, with consent

### 5. Step-by-step teaching content
#### 0:00–0:10 · Recap and hooks you found (10 min, introduction and recap)
*Share the hooks you noticed and check your shot list.*

1. Learners share one hook they noticed in their niche.
2. Check that each learner has a shot list and consent from their brief.

#### 0:10–0:30 · Composition and phone camera basics (20 min, main lesson)
*Learn composition techniques and the camera settings that make the biggest difference.*

1. Demonstrate turning on gridlines (Android and iPhone camera settings). Teach the rule of thirds - place the subject on a line or intersection, not dead centre, unless symmetry is the point.
2. Leading lines (a road, a counter, a row of market stalls) draw the eye to the subject; framing (a doorway, a window, leaves) adds depth.
3. Angles: eye level for connection; low angle to make the subject look powerful; high angle (top-down) for food and products.
4. Clean backgrounds: move the subject or yourself to remove clutter; a plain wall or kanga cloth can make a product pop.
5. Camera basics: clean the lens with a soft cloth (phones in pockets get greasy); tap to focus and hold to lock focus and exposure; slide to adjust brightness; portrait mode for people and products; avoid digital zoom - move closer instead; take several shots and pick the best.
6. Show good and bad examples of each technique, using photos of Kenyan markets, food and people (with consent).

#### 0:30–0:45 · Lighting (15 min, main lesson)
*Use natural light to make photos and videos look professional.*

1. Light is the biggest difference between amateur and professional-looking content. Natural light is free.
2. Window light: place the subject facing or beside a window, not with the window behind them (that creates a silhouette).
3. Time of day: early morning and late afternoon (golden hour) give soft, warm light; midday sun in Kenya is harsh and creates strong shadows - move into open shade.
4. Backlight on purpose: for silhouettes or glowing hair, but expose for the face if you want it visible.
5. DIY tools: a white manila sheet or foil-covered cardboard as a reflector; a white curtain or bedsheet to soften harsh light; a cheap LED ring light for night filming.
6. Demonstrate moving a product from a dark corner to window light and show the difference.

#### 0:45–1:00 · Mobile video production (15 min, main lesson)
*Learn shot types, B-roll, stable filming and simple camera movement.*

1. Orientation: vertical (9:16) for TikTok, Reels, Shorts and status; horizontal (16:9) for YouTube. Decide before filming.
2. Shot types: wide (where are we), medium (who and what are they doing), close-up (detail and emotion). A good sequence mixes them: wide market, medium vendor arranging tomatoes, close-up hands and a smile.
3. B-roll: extra footage that shows what is being talked about (hands working, the street outside, the product). Film more B-roll than you think you need.
4. Stability: two hands, elbows tucked in, lean on a wall, use a cheap tripod or a stack of books. Hold each shot steady for at least 5 seconds so you have room to edit.
5. Movement: slow pan, push in (walk slowly towards the subject), follow (walk behind someone). Move slowly; fast movement looks shaky.
6. Settings: film at 1080p and 30 frames per second as a safe default; check storage before you start.

#### 1:00–1:05 · Stretch break (5 min, stretch break)
*A five-minute stretch and movement break (seated option available).*

1. Invite everyone to stand, stretch, walk or drink water for five minutes; offer a seated option for anyone who prefers or needs it.
2. Restart on time with a one-line reminder of where the session is and what comes next.

#### 1:05–1:15 · Capturing clean sound (10 min, main lesson)
*Record clear voice and avoid the most common sound problems.*

1. People forgive average video but not bad sound. Choose quiet places, away from matatu stages, generators and loud music.
2. Get the mic close: the phone or a wired earphone mic about 20 to 30 cm from the speaker's mouth; a cheap lapel mic improves sound a lot.
3. Avoid wind (shield the mic, record indoors or behind a wall) and echo (rooms with soft furnishings echo less than empty rooms).
4. Always record a 10-second test, listen with earphones, then record.

#### 1:15–1:45 · Practical shoot (30 min, practical exercise)
*Shoot a five-photo set and the clips for your storyboard.*

1. In pairs (they help each other), learners complete two tasks. Task 1 - a five-photo set for their brief: one wide, one medium, one close-up detail, one top-down or low angle, one portrait of a person with consent - each using a different composition technique and good light.
2. Task 2 - film the clips for their storyboard using the shot list, including at least three B-roll shots and one clean audio clip (a short voice line or interview answer).
3. The facilitator circulates and coaches on light, stability and sound. Learners filming off-site for their brief can do task 2 as homework but must shoot practice clips now.
4. Remind learners to ask permission before photographing anyone and not to film people who decline, or children without their parent's or guardian's consent.

2. Each learner chooses one shot to reshoot and notes why.

1. Learners confirm they have their clips and a free editor installed. Pair anyone without footage with a partner's clips or the facilitator's sample footage.
2. Show a before (raw clips) and after (finished 40-second video) to show what they will make today.

#### 0:10–0:30 · Editing principles and demonstration (20 min, demonstration)
*Learn the editing principles and watch a short video edited step by step.*

1. Principles: (1) hook first - start with the most interesting moment, not 'Hi guys'; (2) cut dead time - remove pauses, setup and repeated shots; (3) cut on action - cut when something moves so the edit feels natural; (4) pacing - change shot every 2 to 4 seconds for short-form; (5) sequences - wide, medium, close-up; (6) transitions - simple cuts are best, flashy effects distract.
2. Live edit in a free mobile editor (for example CapCut, InShot or VN): import clips, put them in storyboard order, trim the start and end of each clip, split and delete a mistake, move the best shot to the start, adjust speed of one B-roll shot, add a title text, adjust brightness and contrast slightly.
3. Safe zones: keep text and faces away from the bottom and right edges where platform buttons and captions cover the screen.

#### 0:30–0:55 · Edit your video (25 min, practical exercise)
*Edit your 30 to 60-second video using the principles.*

1. Learners edit their storyboard footage into a 30 to 60-second video, following the principles and their storyboard (they may improve the story while editing).
2. Checkpoint at 15 minutes: the hook is in the first 2 seconds and there is no dead time.
3. The facilitator circulates and helps with the app.

#### 0:55–1:00 · Stretch break (5 min, stretch break)
*A five-minute stretch and movement break (seated option available).*

1. Invite everyone to stand, stretch, walk or drink water for five minutes; offer a seated option for anyone who prefers or needs it.
2. Restart on time with a one-line reminder of where the session is and what comes next.

#### 1:00–1:15 · Audio and sound - editing and legal music (15 min, main lesson)
*Improve sound, add a voice-over and use music legally.*

1. Levels: voice must be clearly louder than music; lower the music under speech (many editors call this ducking or allow manual volume).
2. Noise reduction: use the editor's noise reduction feature if available; it cannot fix very bad recordings, so capture well.
3. Voice-over: record in a quiet room close to the phone, speak slowly and clearly, re-record mistakes.
4. Music licensing: songs belong to their creators and labels. Platforms such as TikTok and Instagram provide music libraries that are licensed for use on that platform within their terms; business accounts may have fewer choices. Royalty-free libraries (for example the YouTube Audio Library) provide music you can use under their terms. Using a popular Kenyan or international song from elsewhere without a licence can get your video muted or removed, or lead to a copyright claim.
5. Learners add music or a voice-over to their video and balance the levels.

#### 1:15–1:30 · Captions and creative writing (15 min, main lesson)
*Add accurate on-screen captions and write a post caption that connects.*

1. On-screen captions: many people watch without sound, and captions make content accessible to people who are deaf or hard of hearing. Use the editor's auto-captions, then correct every error - names, places, Kiswahili and Sheng words are often wrong. Keep two lines maximum, high contrast, inside the safe zone.
2. Post caption structure: hook (first line visible before 'more'), value or story (what the viewer gets, or the story behind the video), call to action (follow, comment, share, visit), and a few relevant hashtags or keywords that people actually search.
3. Creative writing techniques: specific details ('the smell of mandazi at 6 am in Kariokor'), a question to start conversation, humour that fits the audience, and short sentences.
4. Learners add on-screen captions and write the post caption.

#### 1:30–1:45 · Finish and export (15 min, practical exercise)
*Finish your video and export it correctly for your platform.*

1. Final check: hook, no dead time, sound levels, captions correct, text in safe zones, ending with a call to action or logo.
2. Export: 1080p, 30 fps for most platforms; 9:16 for short vertical video; save a copy to the cloud. Remove the editor's watermark only if the app's terms allow it.
3. Learners save the final video in their DIRA LEARN portfolio folder.

#### 1:45–1:55 · Screening and knowledge check (10 min, assessment and feedback)
*Screen a few videos and give feedback.*

1. Screen three or four videos (volunteers). The group gives feedback on the hook, pacing, sound and captions.
2. Ask two or three assessment questions.

#### 1:55–2:00 · Wrap-up and homework (5 min, conclusion and assignment)
*Summarise and confirm the homework.*

1. Set the homework and remind learners to bring their brand ideas to session 6.

#### 0:10–0:30 · Graphic design fundamentals (20 min, main lesson)
*Learn the principles that make graphics look professional.*

1. Teach CRAP with good and bad examples: Contrast (make the important thing different - size, colour, weight); Repetition (repeat colours, fonts and styles so it feels like one design); Alignment (everything lines up with something; use the grid); Proximity (group related items, separate unrelated ones).
2. Typography: maximum two fonts - one for headings, one for body; use size and weight for hierarchy; avoid decorative fonts for long text; check readability at phone size.
3. Colour: a palette of two or three main colours plus neutrals; colours carry meaning (green - fresh, growth; red - urgency, passion); ensure strong contrast between text and background.
4. Grids and hierarchy: decide the one thing viewers should notice first, second and third. Use margins and consistent spacing.
5. Redesign a cluttered event poster live (a youth football tournament poster with five fonts and no hierarchy) into a clean one.

#### 0:30–0:45 · Visual storytelling (15 min, main lesson)
*Tell stories across carousels, posters and covers.*

1. Carousel structure: slide 1 hook (a bold question or promise), slides 2 to 6 the story (steps, before-and-after, tips), last slide payoff and call to action (save, share, follow, visit).
2. Before-and-after: a room makeover, a hairstyle, a cleaned-up river bank; consistent angle makes it powerful.
3. Simple infographics: one idea, few numbers, clear icons; always credit the data source.
4. Thumbnails and covers: a face with emotion, three to five words, high contrast.
5. Posters for events and causes: what, when, where, who, how to join - the 5Ws in hierarchy.

#### 0:45–1:00 · Content branding and consistency (15 min, main lesson)
*Build a brand kit and templates so your content is recognisable.*

1. Explain why consistency matters - people should recognise your content in a busy feed before seeing your name.
2. Brand kit contents: logo or signature mark, two or three colours with codes, two fonts, photo and video style (bright, moody, natural), voice (playful, calm, expert), recurring series ('Cheap Eats Friday'), intro or outro style, and three templates (post, carousel, story).
3. Show 'Otieno Shoots' brand kit as an example.
4. Demonstrate saving a template in Canva (or similar) and reusing it with new content.

#### 1:00–1:05 · Stretch break (5 min, stretch break)
*A five-minute stretch and movement break (seated option available).*

1. Invite everyone to stand, stretch, walk or drink water for five minutes; offer a seated option for anyone who prefers or needs it.
2. Restart on time with a one-line reminder of where the session is and what comes next.

#### 1:05–1:25 · Copyright and responsible content use (20 min, main lesson)
*Learn the legal and ethical rules for using and creating content.*

1. Copyright protects original works - photos, videos, music, designs, writing - from the moment they are created. In Kenya, the Copyright Act sets the rules and the Kenya Copyright Board (KECOBO) administers copyright matters. Owning a copy (downloading a photo) does not give you the right to reuse it.
2. To use others' work you need permission or a licence. Sources: your own content, the brief owner's content with permission, Creative Commons works (follow the licence - attribution, non-commercial, no derivatives), and free stock sites under their licence terms. Keep the licence link in your credit log.
3. Fair dealing (limited uses such as criticism, review or news reporting) exists in many laws, but it is narrow and not a free pass to use songs, films or photos in your content. When in doubt, do not use it or ask permission.
4. Consent: get clear permission before filming or photographing people for content, and written permission (a model release - a simple signed or WhatsApp-confirmed agreement) when the content is for a business or will be used widely. Never post children without their parent's or guardian's consent, and avoid identifying vulnerable people.
5. Responsibility: do not spread misinformation (check facts before posting), do not defame (false statements that damage someone's reputation), avoid hate speech and content that incites violence, follow platform community guidelines, disclose paid partnerships ('#ad', 'paid partnership') and AI-generated content.
6. Protecting your own work: add a subtle signature or watermark on photos, keep original files, and know you can report stolen content to platforms.

- Remind learners not to publish personal phone numbers; use a business number or email.
- Consent and privacy: before using an identifiable person (face, voice or name), a photograph of someone, or a real business's name, logo, prices, customer details or results, the learner gets permission from that person or owner - in writing (a WhatsApp message is enough) for anything shown outside the class. Never children. Learners who cannot or do not wish to publish publicly use the private or simulated option, which is assessed with the same rubric and never marked down.
- Shared devices and low data: learners without a suitable phone shoot and edit in pairs or threes and take turns as camera operator, presenter and editor; plan uploads and downloads for programme Wi-Fi. Never require a paid app, premium template or paid music.

**After the session**
- Check that every learner has a complete project; message those at risk.
- Record attendance.
- Before marking, check that every portfolio submission is a restricted-access link or a DIRA LEARN upload; ask learners to change any public or "anyone with the link" sharing first.

### 15. Homework and follow-up
- Finish your portfolio and project and upload them to DIRA LEARN.
- Practise your showcase presentation twice within three minutes.
- Ask your brief owner for a short comment on the work (a testimonial) if they are happy to give one.
- If you are presenting by recording (a large group, or you cannot present live), record your 3-minute project and portfolio presentation - video, or a voice note with slides for low data - and upload it to DIRA LEARN by the day before session 8.

### 16. How this differs from other tracks
- Every track builds its final project in session 7. The Content Creation project is judged on storytelling, production quality and responsible content use.

## Session 8 (Week 4, 120 minutes): Content showcase

*Present your creative project and portfolio, explain your creative choices, give and receive feedback, pitch yourself and plan your next 30 days of content.*

### 1. Learning purpose
Learners present their creative project and portfolio, explain their creative and ethical choices, and answer questions. They practise pitching themselves as creators to clients and employers, show professional behaviour in a showcase setting and leave with a 30-day content plan.

### 2. Learning objectives
By the end of the session learners can:
- Present the creative project and portfolio clearly in three minutes
- Explain at least one creative choice and one responsible-content choice
- Give and receive feedback professionally using the rubric
- Pitch themselves as a creator with the portfolio as proof
- Write a 30-day content plan

### 3. Topics and subtopics
- **Presenting creative work**
    - Showcase structure
    - Explaining creative choices
    - Answering questions
- **Pitching yourself and your work**
    - Creator self-pitch
    - Sharing your portfolio link professionally
    - Talking about rates and briefs
- **Professional behaviour and workplace readiness**
    - Showcase etiquette
    - Receiving critical feedback
    - Following up with clients and guests
    - Pathways - employment, freelance, creator brand, media and NGO communications
- **Next steps**
    - 30-day content plan
    - Staying consistent

### 4. Time allocation
| Time | Minutes | Segment | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 | Showcase set-up and expectations | Introduction and recap |
| 0:10–0:40 | 30 | Project presentations and questions - block 1 | Assessment and feedback |
| 0:40–0:45 | 5 | Stretch break | Stretch break |
| 0:45–1:15 | 30 | Project presentations and questions - block 2 | Assessment and feedback |
| 1:15–1:25 | 10 | Feedback round | Group activity |

### 5. Step-by-step teaching content
#### 0:00–0:10 · Showcase set-up and expectations (10 min, introduction and recap)
*Learn the showcase order, rubric and etiquette.*

1. Welcome learners and guests (brief owners, DIRA staff, mentors, employers). Thank brief owners.
2. Share the rubric: storytelling and strategy (20%), production quality - photo, video, sound (25%), design and brand consistency (20%), responsible content - credits, consent, honesty (15%), presentation and answers (20%).
3. Assign each learner two presenters to give written feedback to.

#### 0:10–0:40 · Project presentations and questions - block 1 (30 min, assessment and feedback)
*The first six presenters in each room present and answer questions; give rubric feedback to your two assigned peers.*

1. Run the screenings with the session 8 room plan in the facilitator instructions: the first six presenters in each room go in this block, each with 3 minutes plus 2 minutes of questions.
2. Learners present in order, 3 minutes each plus 2 minutes of questions. Play videos with sound checked in advance.
3. Ask at least one question per presenter, such as: 'Why did you open with that shot?', 'What would you change with more time?', 'How did you get consent?', 'What result did this piece get?'
4. Score with the rubric and note one strength and one improvement.

#### 0:40–0:45 · Stretch break (5 min, stretch break)
*A five-minute stretch and movement break (seated option available).*

1. Invite everyone to stand, stretch, walk or drink water for five minutes; offer a seated option for anyone who prefers or needs it.
2. Restart on time with a one-line reminder of where the session is and what comes next.

#### 0:45–1:15 · Project presentations and questions - block 2 (30 min, assessment and feedback)
*The next six presenters in each room present; recorded presenters answer their live questions.*

1. Presenters 7 to 12 in each live room now show their projects and portfolios; check the sound before each video and keep the block 1 timer, rubric and questions.
2. In a recorded-questions room, do not replay the recordings (the panel scored them before the session); give each recorded presenter 2 minutes of live questions about their creative project and portfolio.
3. Each room's panel hands its completed creative project and portfolio rubric sheets to the room facilitator before the feedback round.

#### 1:15–1:25 · Feedback round (10 min, group activity)
*Share feedback and learn from patterns across projects.*

1. Learners give written feedback to their two assigned presenters.
2. Facilitator highlights patterns in strong projects - strong hooks, good light and sound, consistent brand, clear credits.

#### 1:25–1:40 · Pitching yourself and workplace readiness (15 min, main lesson)
*Practise pitching yourself as a creator and learn the pathways for creative work.*

1. Creator pitch formula: who you are, what you make and for whom, proof (a piece and its result), the ask. Example: 'I am Wanjiku, a Nyeri content creator. I make short videos that help small cafes get noticed - my last video for a local cafe got 12,000 views and the owner says weekend visits went up. I am looking for two more cafes to work with this month.'
2. Sharing your portfolio: a short link, a clean index, and a message that introduces you and the value you offer - not 'please check my page'.
3. Pathways: employment (social media assistant, videographer, communications assistant at an NGO or company), freelance briefs, building a creator brand, media houses and production companies. Rates vary; start by agreeing clear deliverables and never paying to get work.
4. Professional follow-up: thank brief owners and guests within two days, deliver final files, ask for a testimonial.
5. Learners practise their pitch once in pairs.

#### 1:40–1:55 · 30-day content plan (15 min, practical exercise)
*Write your plan for the next 30 days of content.*

1. Learners complete the 30-day content plan: main goal, pillars, posting rhythm, four weekly actions (for example week 1 publish two portfolio pieces, week 2 pitch three local businesses, week 3 start a recurring series, week 4 review analytics), a measure, an accountability partner and one risk.
2. Learners share their goal with their accountability partner.

#### 1:55–2:00 · Celebration and close (5 min, conclusion and assignment)
*Celebrate and learn what happens next.*

1. Celebrate each learner's project in one sentence.
2. Explain how to submit the portfolio (a restricted-access link or files uploaded in DIRA LEARN, never a public link), creative project and credit and consent log in DIRA LEARN, how results and certificates follow the programme's rules, and how alumni can share paid briefs with each other.

### 6. Practical demonstrations
- **Model showcase**

### 7. Guided exercises

### 8. Individual activities
- **Showcase presentation:** Present your project and portfolio in three minutes and answer questions for two minutes.
- **30-day content plan:** Complete the plan with goal, pillars, rhythm, weekly actions, measure, partner and risk.

### 9. Group activities
- **Peer feedback:** Give rubric-based written feedback to two assigned presenters.
- **Pitch practice:** Practise your creator pitch in pairs.

### 10. Real-world examples (Kenya)
- A Nyeri creator pitching cafes with a 12,000-view video as proof.
- A Kilifi youth media team showing a conservation clean-up documentary.
- A Kisumu photographer using the portfolio to apply for an NGO communications internship.

### 14. Facilitator instructions
**Before the session**
- Test video playback with sound for every learner before the session; collect files in advance where possible.
- Invite brief owners and guests; brief guests on respectful questions and safeguarding expectations.
- Confirm the room plan: count the learners in the track group, book the rooms and co-facilitators, brief the peer panels, and publish each room's presenter order and the list of recorded presenters by the end of session 7.

**During the session**
- Keep strict time; celebrate creativity and honest reflection.
- Session 8 room plan - Room plan (set in session 7 from the track group size; the session is never extended): up to 12 learners - one room; 13 to 24 - two rooms in parallel; 25 to 36 - three rooms, or two live rooms plus one recorded-questions room. Each live room takes at most 12 presenters: 6 in each 30-minute block, 3 minutes to present and 2 minutes of questions each.
- Every room has a facilitator or briefed co-facilitator (another trainer, a mentor or DIRA staff member who knows the rubric and the safeguarding expectations) and a peer panel of two learners who present in the other block. All rooms use the same rubric.
- Recorded option: learners in a recorded-questions room, and anyone who cannot present live (absence, connection problems, anxiety, or the privacy option), submit a 3-minute recording - video, or a voice note with slides for low data - by the day before session 8. The panel scores it in advance with the same rubric; in session 8 each recorded presenter answers 2 minutes of live questions (12 per block). If more learners need the recorded option than one room can take, the panel sends written questions and the learner answers them in DIRA LEARN.
- Timekeeping: a visible timer with a 30-second warning; stop at 3 minutes. If a block runs late, cut questions to 1 minute - do not extend the session.

**Inclusion and safeguarding**
- Learners who cannot present live can submit a recorded presentation according to the programme's rules.
- Guests must not contact learners privately outside programme channels.
- Shared devices and low data: learners without a suitable phone shoot and edit in pairs or threes and take turns as camera operator, presenter and editor; plan uploads and downloads for programme Wi-Fi. Never require a paid app, premium template or paid music.

**After the session**
- Enter rubric scores and feedback in DIRA LEARN grading.
- Record attendance.
